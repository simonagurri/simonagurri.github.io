This work presents a spark ignition model based on a multi-point thermal power
source for three-dimensional Reynolds-Averaged Navier Stokes (RANS)
computational fluid-dynamics (CFD) simulations of spark ignition (SI) engines.
The aim is providing a tool to simulate the cycle-to-cycle variability (CCV) of
the combustion which can be attributed to the ignition process, at a
computational cost compatible with industrial development. The model accounts
for the three phases of the spark discharge process and modulates the effective
thermal energy deposition based on in-cylinder pressure and electrode gap
distance through experimentally validated conversion efficiency correlations.
The discharge channel is represented as a line source convected and stretched by
the local flow field, enabling the parametric variation of arc geometry and
discharge parameters to reproduce cycle-to-cycle variability deterministically.
The model is validated against experimental in-cylinder pressure traces and mass
fraction burned (MFB) profiles across three representative engine operating
conditions, covering a range of loads and engine speeds and taking into account
CCV. Arc position and orientation are found to control the early flame
development; at low load the model covers more than 95% of experimental MFB10
range and more than 71% of MFB50, whereas the coverage falls to around 20% at
high load where turbulence-induced variability, not resolved by the
ensemble-averaging framework of RANS, and limitations of the kinetic mechanism
become dominant. The proposed approach has a computational cost comparable to
standard energy-deposition models, making it well suited for industrial
applications where throughput is a primary constraint.
